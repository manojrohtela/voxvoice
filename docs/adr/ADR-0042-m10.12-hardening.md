## Status

**Accepted — 2026-08-21**, at the M10.12 architectural decision gate, **before** implementation.

Every prior Epic 10 ADR (0035–0041) was created directly in `Accepted` state because the owner ruled
**at** the decision gate that produced it. This one took the longer route the DOCUMENTATION_RULES §3
item 4 lifecycle provides: drafted as `Proposed`, reviewed against ADRs 0032–0041, the Constitution,
DOCUMENTATION_RULES, TESTING_POLICY and PERFORMANCE_POLICY, corrected on three findings from that
review (HD16 gate ordering, HV6 scope, HV7 feasibility), and only then ratified. The rulings below are
therefore a record of a decision made, not a proposal.

This ADR ratifies the **M10.12 architecture** only. Epics 5–9 remain frozen;
`platform/billing/**` is byte-for-byte unchanged; `platform/api/**`, `platform/runtime/**`,
`platform/observability/**` and `platform/core/**` are unchanged; `MetricTurnBuffer`,
`MetricStreamPort`, `DurableEventLog` and the M10.5 retention contract are unchanged; migrations
`0001`–`0036` are untouched; certification controls, freeze manifests and release artifacts are
untouched; `v4.0.0` is not reopened. **DL-0139 stays Resolved. DL-0141 stays Resolved.**

## Context

### §M10.12 is two lines

The Epic 10 implementation plan says, in full:

> **M10.12 — Hardening.** Performance (latency taps must not violate invariant I1), failure drills,
> operator surfaces (DLQ depth, quarantine count), and the boundary/AST suite extended per milestone.

Four workstreams, one sentence. There is **no design gate, no prerequisite list, no deliverable
enumeration, no acceptance criteria and no non-goals** — and a repository-wide search for a fuller
definition finds only sequencing references (ADR-0035 §260, ADR-0041 §30/§227, plan §64/§222,
DL-0141) and a bare list entry in the Epic 10 blueprint. M10.12 is the **only** Epic 10 milestone
carrying architectural content without a ratified gate, and the omission is not benign: three of its
four workstreams cannot be implemented without interpretation, and one of them would extend a
ratified port contract.

### The primitives already exist

M10.5–M10.11 are delivered and committed. Every failure mode M10.12 would drill, and every datum its
operator surfaces would report, already exists:

| Needed by M10.12 | Already delivered |
|---|---|
| DLQ / quarantine state | `OutboxState.DEAD_LETTERED` / `QUARANTINED`, indexed `evt_outbox.state` (M10.6/M10.7) |
| Count precedent | `DurableOutbox.count_pending()` / `count_entries()` |
| Durable-write failure | `DurableWriteFailures` + `DurableWriteFailureHook` (ADR-0035 DW4) |
| Retry ladder + terminal states | `OutboxLadder`, `OUTBOX_LIFECYCLE` (ADR-0036) |
| Recovery-path failure | `OutboxRecovery` (ADR-0037) |
| Observer failure isolation | M10.9 tap tests |
| Retention retain-on-raise | `EventRetention` RT8 (ADR-0041) |
| Hermetic scenario-suite precedent | `tests/test_epic10_shadow_validation.py` (ADR-0040 SV5/SV7) |

M10.12 therefore adds **no new capability**. It hardens what exists — which is exactly what the
repository's earlier hardening milestones did (`test_production_hardening.py`, M8.18: *"No new
features — hardening only."*).

### Three ambiguities, and the evidence that settles them

**Performance.** *"Latency taps must not violate invariant I1"* reads two ways. The structural
reading is already discharged: **ADR-0038** ruled that *"boundary capture is a clock read and a dict
write… publication happens once, after the turn is already terminal"*, so added latency **cannot**
reach the caller — Blueprint risk **R-9** (*"latency taps violate I1"*) was closed by design at M10.9,
not deferred here. The quantitative reading is not available to M10.12: PERFORMANCE_POLICY §1 states
budgets are *"measured by the `metric.turn` taps (AR-42) — production telemetry and benchmarks use the
**same** instrumentation"*, which means real turns through real providers (**Epic 11**);
PERFORMANCE_POLICY §5.1 gates the benchmark at *"Stage 2+"* and requires comparison *"against the last
baseline"*; the Constitution repeats that gate (*"CI-enforced from Stage 2"*); and **`docs/performance/`
does not exist**, so no baseline has ever been recorded and §5.1 has no operand. TESTING_POLICY §2
independently forbids the shape such a test would take (*"No sleeps for synchronization… A test that
needs a sleep has found a design defect"*).

**Failure drills.** The term appears **exactly once in the repository** — in the sentence being
interpreted. It has no definition in the plan, the blueprint, any ADR or the decision log.

**Operator surfaces.** *"DLQ depth, quarantine count"* names two numbers that no contract exposes.
`DurableOutbox` counts entries and pending rows and nothing else. Exposing them extends a ratified
port, under ADR-0036 **RL3**/**RL4**, which defer redrive and operator quarantine to *"a future
operator-control milestone… only under separate authorization"*. Read-only counting is plausibly
compatible with that deferral — but *plausibly* is not a standard, and the boundary against Epic 12's
read API must be drawn rather than assumed.

## Decision

### HD1 — Performance means structural assurance, not quantitative measurement

M10.12's performance obligation is discharged **structurally**. ADR-0038's post-terminal publication
ruling and its TP10 proof are the **architectural baseline**; M10.12 re-implements and re-proves
neither. What M10.12 adds is **static guards** that prevent the property from regressing silently — a
publish call migrating to a non-terminal boundary, or a blocking construct entering the tap module,
must fail a guard rather than a production incident.

### HD2 — No wall-clock benchmark and no production p50/p95 at M10.12

**No latency benchmark, timing assertion, or production percentile measurement is part of M10.12.**
Real p50/p95 requires the taps against real providers (Epic 11), a `docs/performance/` baseline that
does not exist, and a Stage-2 gate that is not active. A wall-clock assertion would additionally
contradict TESTING_POLICY §2 and the standing requirement that the suite stay deterministic and
hermetic.

### HD3 — I1 remains a standing Epic 11 obligation

**M10.12 does not claim that production latency has been measured.** Constitution **I1** says
*"unmeasured latency is a defect"*, and voice-to-voice latency **is** currently unmeasured. That is
recorded here as an open obligation belonging to Epic 11 and the Stage-2 benchmark regime — not
absorbed, not silently satisfied, and not closed by this ADR.

### HD4 — A failure drill is a test scenario, never a production feature

A **failure drill** is a **deterministic, hermetic, caller-driven test scenario** that composes
already-defined failure behaviour end-to-end across milestone boundaries. It lives in
`platform/tests/**` and ships no production code. The value it adds is composition: each failure mode
is already unit-tested in isolation, and nothing today proves they behave correctly *together*.

The M10.11 shadow-validation suite is the ratified precedent — real contracts, real implementations,
hermetic, wiring no production component (ADR-0040 SV5/SV7).

### HD5 — Drills introduce nothing into production

Failure drills introduce **no new production state, transition, trigger, API, scheduler, thread,
timer, background task or `sleep`**. Caller-driven only, consistent with **ADR-0037 RC2** and the
repository's `context`/`memory`/`OutboxRecovery` convention.

### HD6 — Drill coverage is limited to already-defined failure modes

Exactly these, all already implemented:

1. **durable-write failure** (ADR-0035 DW4) — producer never raised into, event never dispatched;
2. **transient delivery failure** → `RETRYING` with an absolute `next_attempt_at`;
3. **retry exhaustion** → `DEAD_LETTERED` (ED7);
4. **permanent failure** → `QUARANTINED`, ladder not consumed (ED8);
5. **recovery-path failure** — classifier-driven outcome, terminal entries never re-selected;
6. **observer failure** — a raising subscriber never breaks the conversation;
7. **retention retain-on-raise** — ADR-0041 **RT8** default-deny under an unavailable guard.

No failure mode outside this list is drilled, and no new one is invented.

### HD7 — Operator surfaces are exactly two read-only counts

`DurableOutbox` gains exactly two methods:

```
count_dead_lettered() -> int
count_quarantined()   -> int
```

Nothing else. No depth histogram, no age percentile, no listing, no pagination, no stream, no
subscription.

### HD8 — Count semantics, fixed

Both counts are **point-in-time**, **total**, **not windowed**, **not session-scoped**, **read-only**,
derived from the existing **`evt_outbox.state`** column, and served by the existing
`ix_evt_outbox_state_seq` index. They mirror `count_pending()` exactly.

The absences are deliberate: `evt_outbox` carries no session column, so session scoping would require
a join or a migration, and `count_pending()` takes no window, so adding one here would invent a
dimension the model does not carry.

### HD9 — Observability, not operator control

These are **read-only observability surfaces**. **No redrive, retry, release, drain, delete,
quarantine mutation, state transition or operator-triggered action is introduced.** A caller holding
both counts still has no way to move an entry out of a terminal state.

**ADR-0036 RL3 and RL4 remain fully in force** and the *"future operator-control milestone"* they
defer remains deferred and unentered. Counting rows performs no transition, so it is not the thing
those rulings forbid.

### HD10 — `DurableOutbox` is the authoritative location

The counts belong on `DurableOutbox`, implemented once by `SqlDurableEventSpine`. **No module outside
`events/persistence/` may query `evt_outbox` directly.** A reader that reached the table itself would
bypass the port and become a second implementation of an existing contract — the drift ADR-0034's
storage seam exists to prevent.

### HD11 — No migration

**No database migration.** `evt_outbox.state` exists (migration `0035`), has carried `dead_lettered`
and `quarantined` since `0036`, and is indexed both alone and as `(state, seq)`. Both counts are index
scans against the current schema. **The chain remains at `0036`.**

### HD12 — No production wiring

**No orchestrator, Session Worker, transport, HTTP endpoint, ops route, scheduler, background process
or composition-root change.** Consistent with **DW10 / RL17 / RC16 / RT9**, which keep the durable
runtime deliberately unwired. M10.12 delivers hardening and its proofs, and wires nothing.

### HD13 — The load-bearing contracts are unchanged

**`DurableEventLog`** keeps exactly its five methods. **M10.5 retention semantics** — the RT7
safe-to-delete predicate, RT8 default-deny, `DELIVERED` as the only drained state — are unchanged.
**EV4**, **EV8** and **SV9** are unchanged. The **`events → billing`** prohibition holds, and
**PL-2 / IR4** are not widened.

### HD14 — Guards are extended additively and never weakened

Existing guards — the `DurableOutbox` deny-list, the EV4 deletion allowlist, the boundary/AST suite,
the retention and shadow-validation suites — are **extended only**. **No existing test may be deleted,
skipped, xfailed, narrowed or rewritten to accommodate M10.12.** If an existing guard would reject the
intended implementation, implementation **STOPS and reports** rather than changing the guard.

### HD15 — Out of scope

**M10.13, M10.14, M10.15, Epic 11 and Epic 12 are out of scope.** In particular: no certification
control set, no freeze manifest, no release artifact, no production latency measurement, and no
read API. M10.12 must not become a vehicle for functionality none of its four workstreams names.

### HD16 — Gates that follow

1. **ADR acceptance / ratification** — the owner decision moving ADR-0042 from `Proposed` to
   `Accepted`;
2. **ADR registration** — add the **Accepted** ADR to `docs/INDEX.md` and `docs/adr/README.md`, as
   required by DOCUMENTATION_RULES §3 item 6;
3. **M10.12 implementation** — separately authorized, within the exact scope above;
4. **M10.12 validation** — separately authorized;
5. **M10.12 commit** — separately authorized.

Registration follows acceptance, not the reverse: `docs/INDEX.md` carries a **Status** column in which
every registered ADR reads `Accepted`, so registering a `Proposed` document would put a status into
that index which the document does not hold.

**Each gate is its own authorization. None is granted by this document, and drafting it grants
nothing at all.**

## Rejected interpretations

| # | Interpretation | Why rejected |
|---|---|---|
| **HR1** | **Quantitative latency benchmarking inside M10.12** | Production latency measurement belongs to **Epic 11** and the **Stage-2** benchmark regime; PERFORMANCE_POLICY §5.1 requires comparison against a baseline, and **`docs/performance/` does not exist**, so no baseline has ever been recorded. TESTING_POLICY §2 independently forbids the test shape. |
| **HR2** | **A production operator endpoint or ops API** | §M10.12 names **counts**, not an interface. An HTTP/ops surface is **Epic 12's read API** (HD15), and no M10.12 text authorizes production wiring (HD12). |
| **HR3** | **Redrive, retry or quarantine control** | **ADR-0036 RL3/RL4** — no redrive API, no operator-driven quarantine; both explicitly deferred to a separately authorized operator-control milestone. |
| **HR4** | **New failure states or a new recovery mechanism** | Drills **compose existing behaviour** (HD4/HD6). A new state would change `OUTBOX_LIFECYCLE` and require its own ADR; a new recovery mechanism would be the second competing one blueprint risk **R-11** exists to prevent. |
| **HR5** | **Scheduler or background drill execution** | **ADR-0037 RC2** — caller-driven only. *When* anything runs is a deployment concern, and a drill that scheduled itself would be non-deterministic besides. |
| **HR6** | **Direct `evt_outbox` queries outside the persistence contract** | Bypasses `DurableOutbox`, creating a second implementation of a ratified contract and dissolving the ADR-0034 storage seam (HD10). |
| **HR7** | **A database migration** | `evt_outbox.state` and its indexes already support both counts exactly; a migration would add schema to buy nothing (HD11). |
| **HR8** | **Treating M10.12 as "hardening, therefore anything"** | The repository's hardening precedent is explicit — M8.18: *"No new features — hardening only."* M10.12 is bounded by its four named workstreams. |

| # | Proof |
|---|---|

| **HV2** | `count_quarantined()` likewise, across every state, including zero. |
| **HV3** | Both counts **mutate nothing** — outbox rows, event rows and failure history are byte-identical before and after, and the counts are stable across repeated calls. |

| **HV7** | No M10.12 drill module and no module M10.12 adds or modifies contains `time.sleep`, `asyncio.sleep`, `threading`, `Timer`, a polling loop or a scheduler construct. | **HV9** | Boundary/AST guards prevent regression of **post-terminal latency publication** — a publish at a non-terminal boundary fails a guard. |

## Consequences

**Gained.** M10.12 becomes implementable against a written scope instead of two lines of prose · the
post-terminal latency property acquires a regression guard rather than resting on a single existing
test · the failure modes M10.6–M10.9 and M10.5 built in isolation acquire end-to-end proofs · an
operator can finally see DLQ depth and quarantine count without a database console · the boundary/AST
suite catches up with M10.5 and M10.9.

**Accepted cost.** The two counts extend a ratified port, which is a real contract change, justified
because the alternative — reading `evt_outbox` from outside the seam — is worse. Drills add test
runtime without adding features.

## Known limitations

Recorded plainly rather than implied by silence:

* **M10.12 does not measure real production p50/p95.** Nothing here asserts that I1 holds in production.
* **I1 remains an open production / Stage-2 measurement obligation**, owned by Epic 11.
* **M10.12 creates no operator control** — no redrive, no release, no requeue, no manual transition.
* **M10.12 provides no HTTP or ops endpoint**; the counts are in-process port methods only.
* **M10.12 does not create Epic 12's read API**, and must not be treated as a first instalment of it.
* **Failure drills prove nothing about external-provider production reliability** — they are hermetic
  and use injected fakes, so they prove the platform's *handling* of failure, not any vendor's behaviour.
* **No new recovery mechanism is introduced**; recovery remains exactly ADR-0037's caller-driven sweep.
* Counts are point-in-time totals; trend, age and per-session breakdown are **not** available and would
  require a model the schema does not carry.
